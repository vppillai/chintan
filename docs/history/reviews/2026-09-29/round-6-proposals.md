## 5. The checklist strategy

**What is wrong today, in one sentence:** the system has two checklist prompts that both keep the person's words, a parser that can only carry flat lines, and an append that only ever adds — so a spoken list becomes verbatim sentences, groups become part of an item's words or vanish, and a second recording about Costco starts a second Costco.

**The evidence.** Your 29 Sept Shopping list (row 1 of §1) never reached the items prompt — the router made a plain note. The per-recording extraction already gives Milk · Eggs · Protein powder for that sentence when the recording reaches a checklist (prod battery row 1), so #134's `kind` closes that half. The prod battery (`r6/checklist/prod-battery.md`, 15 utterances targeted at an "R6 Shopping list" on the test tenant, purged) scores **10 of 15** today, and every failure is grouping: "eggs from Walmart and meat from Costco" → `Eggs from Walmart`, `Meat from Costco`; "from Costco … and from the Indian store …" → five flat items; "for the party plates, cups and napkins" → three flat; the wall of speech → six flat (fillers gone); the ring's "Shopping list eggs from Walmart" → `Eggs from Walmart`. You nest by hand already ("Things to talk with Milos": 10 lines, 5 sub-items in the two days since #130).

**The strategy (S1 + S4), in five moves.**

1. **Items are a one-level tree, in both prompts.** Model reply `{"items":[{"text":"Walmart","children":[{"text":"Eggs"}]},{"text":"Milk"}]}`; Split up adds `"done":true`. Go `cleanup.Item{Text; Done; Children}`. A grandchild is clamped to a child of the top-level item (CL-D1 stands); a bare string is still an item, so a model that answers the old way degrades to flat, never to unusable. Rendered to the body as the GFM lines the frontend already reads and writes (children two spaces in) — no wire, schema or store change.
2. **One rule block, `checklistItemRules`, shared by the items prompt and the Split up prompt:** what an item is; app words out, including a spoken title prefix ("Shopping list eggs from Walmart" → Walmart › Eggs); split X and Y; **group as the person grouped — a place, person, occasion or category the things are named under is the parent, one level, never invented**; remove/tick requests returned as spoken; fix garbling, drop fillers, lose nothing. The items wrapper carries six examples, your two sentences first. The Split up wrapper is "the list as it stands → the list it was meant to be": keep every line's meaning, split sentences spoken to the app, keep and join existing groups, merge duplicates with open winning, done stays done; its user prompt names the list's title so the list's own name is never an item (the Business ideas case).
3. **Guards that check instead of trust.** Per recording: object or string items, ≤100 counting children, ≤2,000 runes each, an empty parent lifts its children. Split up: every item's words a sub-sequence of the body (nothing invented — a dropped parent lifts its children); **tick safety**: every `- [x]` body line accounted for by a done answer item, else the whole answer is refused; an open answer item equal to a done body line refused unless the body also has an open duplicate. The old "done lines verbatim and in order" rule goes: it is what forced "Add milk to shopping list" to survive a split.
4. **Merge on append respects what is already there.** A parent that matches an existing top-level line joins that block (new children after the block's last line, a done child named again reopened, a done parent that gained a child reopened); a childless item that matches any line is not added again (open → dropped, done → reopened, and a reopened sub-item reopens its parent — "add milk" over a ticked Milk means milk is wanted again); everything else goes under the recording's marker as today. Ticks only ever flip `[x]→[ ]`. Documented limit: merged sub-items live under their parent, so "delete this recording" removes what is under its marker only; Regenerate dedupes against the parent, so nothing doubles. Oracle: `r6/checklist/merge/merge.go`, 12 cases green.
5. **Split up is the Items editor over the proposal, and the grip nests.** The tab renders the real `ChecklistEditor` (rows, grips, boxes, Tab, menu, add row, Done); the first act adopts, with Undo for six seconds (R6-CL-D1). Hold the grip and move sideways: the first 10 px decide the axis, 24 px per level, an accent bar previews, release writes once; →/← on a focused grip do the same; the first open row never nests (`canNest`, live since #130).

**The gate.** `fixtures.json` gains 17 items cases (your 15 utterances plus two safety cases) and 8 tasks cases; `LIVE_LLM=1 … -run 'TestLiveEval/(items|tasks)' -count=3` with your key, then the prod battery three times at 15/15.

**Deliberately not built:** depth two; applying "remove milk" / "tick off eggs" to the list (still returned as spoken); a "Tidy up" button replacing the tab (you said keep Split up); parent-name synonyms in the merge ("Costco" vs "Costco wholesale" are two parents — a `ponytail:` ceiling in `append.go`).

##### 1. Details sheet — root cause found and reproduced (CONFIRMED)

The owner: "sometimes the underlying note scrolls and sometimes the details scroll". It is not scroll chaining. It is **`usePullToRefresh` arming on any touch that lands anywhere in `.app__main` while the note is at `scrollTop 0` — including a touch that starts inside the Details sheet**. Evidence (T1, `e2e-r6/details-scroll.spec.ts`, both 412×915 and 412×700): Details open, sheet scrolled to its end (scrollTop 166 / 295), note at 0; a 150 px downward drag on the sheet → mid-gesture `.pull-refresh[data-phase="armed"]`, `--pull-offset: 90px`, `.app__main.scrollHeight` +90 (the note visibly pushed down), the sheet's scrollTop unchanged; on release the note refetches. Dragging the sheet *up* works, and once the note is scrolled a few pixels both directions work — hence "sometimes". ##### 2. The caret under the keyboard — mechanism understood, fix verified in Chromium

Chrome on Android (≥108, `interactive-widget=resizes-visual` default) and iOS both keep the **layout** viewport at full height when the keyboard opens; only the visual viewport shrinks. `.app` is `100dvh`, so `.app__main`'s bottom edge (y≈822 on a 915 layout) sits *under* a ~400 px keyboard. The browser's own caret reveal scrolls the nearest scroller so the caret rect touches that edge and no further (K3: after typing at the end, textarea bottom 835 = main bottom 822 + 13 px padding), then depends on a visual-viewport pan to bring it above the keyboard — which leaves the caret flush against the keyboard's top (0 px margin, the next line hidden) on Android and does not reliably follow typing in a nested scroller on WebKit. That is the inconsistency.

Fix (R6-NAV-2): tell the scroll container where the keyboard is. A 20-line `useKeyboardInset` writes `--keyboard-inset = innerHeight − visualViewport.height − visualViewport.offsetTop` on `<html>`; CSS gives `.app__main` that much `padding-block-end` (room to scroll the end of the note above the keyboard) **and** `scroll-padding-block-end` (the browser's reveal target moves above it). Under `resizes-content` emulation (K1, viewport shrunk to 515) native reveal also works, but that meta is Android-only and would put the tab bar with its 92 px record disc on top of the keyboard (a fifth of the ~500 px left); not taken.
