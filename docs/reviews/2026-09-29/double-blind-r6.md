## 1. Method and agreement

Each area had a Reviewer A and a Reviewer B given the same brief — the range, the specification, the conventions the project enforces, the orb mirror recipe — and no sight of each other's report. Each filed findings with file, line, severity, evidence and a fix. The reconciler for the area deduplicated the two reports, re-verified or refuted every claim, ruled on severity where the two disagreed, and marked each fix *obvious* (mechanical; no decision, no ambiguity in where it goes) or not. Overlap is the share of confirmed distinct defects that both reviewers reached.

What the numbers say. Overlap on the code areas is 22–40 % and on the documentation area 50 %, and the pattern is the same in each: the two reviewers converge on the nits and the observability gaps and diverge on the defects that only a trace or a run exposes. Of the six Mediums, two were found by both reviewers (DB6-2, DB6-6) and four by one alone — the takeover paragraph rewrite (B, who executed the append merges), the span growth that empties a short dictation (A, who hand-traced the routing guards), the Undo over a refetch-reset draft (A, who traced the editor's save timing) and the iOS focus zoom (B, who traced platform behaviour). Each reviewer of the note-screen area missed the other's most consequential defect. The low overlap therefore reads as complementary depth, not shallow review, and is the case for two reviewers per area rather than one. Four claims were refuted, one per reviewer in two areas and two in the documentation area; none of the refutations changed a confirmed finding.
